Why Special Tokens ExistSpecial tokens act as metadata markers that tell a neural network how to handle text structure, boundaries, and alignment during training and generation.

<PAD> (Padding Token)The Problem: Neural networks require data to be passed in fixed-size rectangular matrices (batches). However, real-world sentences have completely different lengths.The Purpose: It acts as a meaningless placeholder appended to shorter sentences to pad them out to match the length of the longest sentence in the batch.Model Action: The network uses an attention mask to explicitly ignore these tokens so they do not influence the gradients or final predictions.

<UNK> (Unknown Token)The Problem: A vocabulary cannot contain every single word in existence (e.g., rare names, typos, or new slang).The Purpose: It acts as a catch-all safety net for any out-of-vocabulary word encountered during text processing.Model Action: Instead of crashing or ignoring the word entirely, the tokenizer substitutes the missing word with <UNK>, allowing the network to maintain the structural rhythm of the sentence.

<BOS> / <s> (Beginning of Sequence Token)The Problem: During text generation, an autoregressive model predicts the next word based on previous words. For the very first word, there is zero historical context to read from.The Purpose: It acts as a standardized starting signal or "trigger" that tells the model to begin generating text out of nothing.Model Action: It sets up the internal states of the model to prepare for structured decoding.

<EOS> / </s> (End of Sequence Token)The Problem: Without an explicit stopping signal, a text-generating model would loop infinitely, spitting out words forever until it runs out of memory.The Purpose: It marks the definitive end of a sentence, paragraph, or document structure.Model Action: During training, the model learns when a thought is complete. During inference, as soon as the model predicts the <EOS> token as its next word, the system automatically halts generation.
